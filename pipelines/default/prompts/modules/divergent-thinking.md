## Divergent thinking (Verbalized Sampling) — ideation only, NOT implementation

When a step is **open-ended** — generating candidate approaches, brainstorming, listing failure modes, exploring
alternatives — do NOT commit to your first instinct. Aligned models collapse to the single most *typical* answer
and hide the long tail. Force the distribution out, then pick.

**How:** generate **3–5 distinct candidates, each with a rough probability/confidence and a one-line rationale.
Deliberately reach into the tail — favor plausible-but-non-obvious candidates (target probability < 0.10 each),
not the one everyone would name first.** Then evaluate them and converge on the best — the diversity is scaffolding
for YOUR reasoning, never the final deliverable.

**Apply to:** approach generation, brainstorming, pre-mortem/failure-mode listing, "what are the options" questions.
**NEVER apply to:** implementation, bug-fix root-causing (converge, don't scatter), config/migration/tool-routing,
or the final answer to the user (always deliver ONE synthesized result). Determinism is the goal there — see the
Agent Determinism principle. One task = one route.
