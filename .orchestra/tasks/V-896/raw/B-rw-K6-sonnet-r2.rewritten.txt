A personal assistant bot receives the owner's voice notes in Telegram, turns them into text, files them into a notes vault and answers questions about them later.

Write: a product name (one word, pronounceable in Russian and English), a tagline of at most eight words, and a three-sentence pitch that makes a sceptical friend want to try it. At most 90 words in total.

Work like a professional namer and positioning writer, and do the work before answering:

1. Start from raw material, not summaries. Use real voice-note transcripts if any exist in this directory (otherwise write a few realistic raw ones, with hesitations and Russian/English code-switching). Also use real sceptic objections (privacy, transcription errors, "I never revisit notes"), in plain words.
2. Generate at least 40 name candidates. Score them against criteria you write down first: easy to say in both languages, one obvious spelling, survives voice dictation, no bad meaning or near-homophone in Russian or English, short.
3. Check the finalists:
   - Say each one in IPA or transliteration in both languages.
   - Do a dictation round trip with a speech-to-text tool, if one is available.
   - Check meanings in Wiktionary or the Russian National Corpus.
   - Check trademark (WIPO, Rospatent, USPTO), domain, and Telegram handle availability.
   Do each check with a real tool where one is available. If a check is impossible here, say which one in a note before the answer, not inside it.
4. Write the pitch around one concrete pain the sceptic already feels. Answer their strongest objection in it. Make each of the three sentences do a different job.
5. Verify with something other than your own judgment: count words with a tool (`wc -w`), and cold-read the pitch as three different sceptical personas, noting what each would reject. Revise until none would.

Final answer: only the name, the tagline, and the three-sentence pitch, with nothing else. Put all notes about checks you could not run before the answer, not inside it.
