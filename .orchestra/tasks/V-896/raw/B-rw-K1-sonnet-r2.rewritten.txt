Write the narration script for a 75-second explainer video for non-technical viewers, titled 'Why an AI agent forgets after a long session, and what a summary does about it'.

Facts you may use (make no claims beyond these; anything else must be clearly metaphor): an agent works in a long conversation; when the conversation grows too big for the model's window it is compressed into a summary and work continues from that; details that were not carried into the summary are gone; a good summary is checked by asking the agent questions about the old work.

Method:
- Before writing, check the working directory for real source material (transcripts, summaries, docs). If any exists, base the example on it; if none, say nothing about it and use only the facts above. Do not invent evidence.
- Budget time first: about 150 words per minute means roughly 185-190 spoken words for 75 seconds. Scene directions count toward the 260-word hard cap, so keep the total at or under 260 words with the spoken narration near 185.
- Pick one concrete everyday metaphor, check that it fits all four facts, and use it throughout. Do not mix metaphors.
- Give each part one job: hook, the problem, what the summary does, what gets lost, how a good summary is checked, takeaway.
- Address likely misconceptions: the AI is not lazy or broken, and it does not remember the way a person does.
- Use plain words at a reading level of about Flesch 70 or higher. Do not use jargon such as "token", "context window" or "LLM" unless explained in plain words.
- Verify before finishing: count the words with a tool, and trace each sentence to one of the four facts or mark it as metaphor, then fix any sentence that does neither. Keep this tracing out of the final answer.

Deliver: the narration with a one-line, producible scene direction before each part, at most 260 words in total. The final answer is only the script.
