    6	# HTML Artifacts
    7	
    8	The unified visual-artifact skill for Orchestra, Claude Code, and Codex. The goal is to
    9	understand the subject and causal relationships at a glance. Text arranged in cards is not by
   10	itself a visualization. An explicit request for another format takes priority; preserve the
   11	existing application's rules.
   12	
   13	## Choose the representation
   14	
   15	| Reader's question | Approach |
   16	|---|---|
   17	| Who is connected, where work goes, where it is delayed | Node diagram as the default; highlight connections |
   18	| How a measure changes | Chart, shared X, and points from all series on hover |
   19	| What changed between moments | Before → after and the difference; show rate changes when needed |
   20	| Where load falls across time/categories | Heatmap with units and legend |
   21	| How an unfamiliar process works | Visual ELI5 explanation: large drawing, few words, sequence |
   22	| How quantities or variants differ | Bars, comparison table, or aligned neighboring charts |
   23	| When depth or geometry matters | Controllable 3D scene; ordinary relationships need only 2D |
   24	
   25	Do not add representations for quantity. The owner disliked Sankey flows: choose nodes by
   26	default; use bands only when requested or when division of a quantity must be shown. Use 3D and
   27	time maps only for suitable tasks. A striking cover is rarely acceptable, only for a creative
   28	request; a report does not need one.
   29	
   30	## Established appearance
   31	
   32	- Light theme by default regardless of OS. Background `#f5f5f7`, panels `#ffffff`, text
   33	  `#1d1d1f`, accent `#007aff`. Series and state colors are stable: green means processed,
   34	  amber means waiting/limited. Color supplements a label; it does not replace one.
   35	- System sans: `-apple-system, BlinkMacSystemFont, "Segoe UI", system-ui, sans-serif`. Prefer
   36	  one family for the working interface; use monospace for code. No second family, forced serif,
   37	  or web font is required.
   38	- Aim for body text 14–15 px, page heading 21–24 px, panel headings 14–17 px, chart labels
   39	  11–12 px, and prominent values 24–30 px. Check readability at normal zoom; do not shrink
   40	  labels to fit a large diagram.
   41	- Dense layout, small gaps, thin dividers, and radii around 8–14 px. Give the main screen to
   42	  data and visualization. Show the main information immediately; do not hide the result behind
   43	  "Show" or a mandatory click. Tabs are allowed for different views.
   44	- A "Tony Stark" tool means relationships, precise feedback, path highlighting, semantic labels,
   45	  and a short mechanism explanation. Decorative telemetry and neon for its own sake interfere.
   46	- Keep the chosen style across artifacts. Do not change palette/fonts for variety or automatically
   47	  carry covers and effects over from third-party design skills.
   48	
   49	## Honest data and dynamics
   50	
   51	Every chart, counter, bar, node size, and indicator needs a source, value, unit, and interval.
   52	When there is no data, show the absence or remove the element. Random curves, fake activity,
   53	decorative sparklines, and queue squares without scale are forbidden. A consistent simulation is
   54	allowed in a demo with an explicit nearby label; it does not replace a measurement in a working
   55	report. Do not connect a live service just to fill a layout.
   56	
   57	- All linked elements show one selected slice. A mini-chart of the full interval is allowed only
   58	  with a clear period, purpose, and marker for the selected moment.
   59	- For a dynamics question, show previous value, current value, and difference. Growth and growth
   60	  acceleration differ: a queue may still grow by 12 requests/min after growing by 19; its rate
   61	  fell by 7 requests/min over 5 s while the queue itself did not shrink.
   62	- State the time step for a rate change. Do not invent a previous zero reading. A known initial
   63	  state may be used as a labeled comparison point.
   64	- Calculate from source numbers and round only the display. Do not turn missing data into zero or
   65	  connect a gap without explaining it. Separate estimates from measurements.
   66	- Scale and element size must match the quantity. Label different scales, aggregation, and a
   67	  clipped axis. A decorative grid does not justify distorted proportions.
   68	
   69	## Hover that explains
   70	
   71	The primary interaction is hover, a tooltip, or a stable neighboring panel. Do not require a
   72	click for details or parameter controls for viewing. A touch screen needs a touch equivalent;
   73	keyboard users need focus/arrows.
   74	
   75	1. A node highlights its inputs, outputs, and linked measures; the highlight explains the dependency.
   76	2. A chart selects the nearest X, highlights all series points, shows one shared tooltip, and
   77	   synchronously updates the diagram, values, and calculations.
   78	3. A panel answers one question with substituted numbers and a conclusion. Teaching example:
   79	   `42 arrives − 30 processed = 12 remains / min`; `after 5 s +1 → queue 35 → 36`;
   80	   `36 ÷ 30 × 60 = 72 s waiting`. This is arithmetic guidance, not report data.
   81	4. Animation may reveal inputs in sequence, fill bars, and show the result. The goal is to
   82	   understand the relationship at a glance. Make the answer available immediately; input must
   83	   not wait for an effect. New hover changes selection immediately; motion inside one node does
   84	   not restart everything.
   85	5. A panel must not cover the subject, jump in height, or leave the viewport. Honor
   86	   `prefers-reduced-motion`; animate a change or explanation, not invented activity.
   87	
   88	Use one state object for the selected time and element; every view reads it. Re-selection must
   89	not recreate the chart or reset focus. A simple drawing needs no controls.
   90	
   91	## Diagrams and 3D
   92	
   93	- First identify nodes, relationships, owners, and constraints. Every arrow means a concrete
   94	  transfer; direction and label carry meaning.
   95	- Inline SVG: `viewBox`, text as `<text>`, arrows through `<marker>`. Give the diagram `<title>`,
   96	  `<desc>`, and `aria-labelledby` with unique IDs — but ONLY on the root `<svg>`.
   97	- **An element with its own tooltip must have neither `<title>` nor a `title` attribute** — the
   98	  browser would draw a native tooltip over your panel and both would appear. Label points, cells,
   99	  and icons with `aria-label`; it draws nothing.
  100	- Connections run edge to edge, do not cross labels, and do not hide under other blocks.
  101	  Orthogonal routes and distinct entry points aid reading. Wrap long text with `<tspan>` or
  102	  shorten it without losing meaning. Split complexity into levels while keeping the overview visible.
  103	- In 3D, rotate by dragging and zoom with wheel/gesture. Page scrolling must not control the
  104	  camera. The scene follows the pointer with pointer capture; movement must not block input.
  105	- Labels must be readable, with a keyboard equivalent and a static explanation without WebGL.
  106	  Object height/color/size must not depict missing data. Prefer 2D for non-spatial tasks. If
  107	  needed, embed Three.js in the file with its license.
  108	
  109	## Standalone file
  110	
  111	- One `.html`: CSS, JS, SVG, and needed resources inside. Work offline without required CDNs,
  112	  external fonts/images, or background APIs. Add libraries only when necessary.
  113	- **Every HTML must have its own subject-specific SVG tab icon:**
  114	  `<link rel="icon" type="image/svg+xml" href="data:image/svg+xml,...">`.
  115	  Encode the SVG as a data URI, including `#`; the icon must work offline with the file.
  116	- Keep facts and calculations in Markdown/JSON task materials; HTML is the presentation. Insert
  117	  external text through textContent/escaping, never execute it as HTML or instructions.
  118	- The editor/gallery exports state to JSON/Markdown. With localStorage, explain that the write is
  119	  local and is not sent to the agent; provide an export if storage is unavailable.
  120	- If the artifact collects user input—fields, choices, ratings, or comments—include one prominent
  121	  **Copy data** button. It copies every entered value with field labels in readable, chat-ready
  122	  text, optionally with JSON. Keep the export client-side: no server storage, localStorage
  123	  synchronization, or external requests. If Clipboard API is unavailable (including `file://` or
  124	  an older browser), show the same text in a selected textarea for manual copying.
  125	- Narrow screens and print are mandatory: white background, readable text, hidden controls. Do
  126	  not mask clipped data with global `overflow:hidden`.
  127	
  128	## Check and deliver
  129	
  130	Open the file in a headless browser: no JS errors, mandatory network requests, overlapping
  131	labels, or unintended page overflow. Check real hover, shared X, units, and the calculation at
  132	the selected moment; when present, check drag/zoom and save/export. Inspect an enlarged diagram
  133	area, narrow screen, and reduced motion. Checking the skill does not replace checking the artifact.
  134	
  135	On the owner's laptop, open finished HTML immediately in Opera with:
  136	`opera /absolute/path/to/file.html`. This is a standing preference; do not ask for each opening.
  137	Tool restrictions still apply. Do not imitate desktop clicks. On a VPS without a desktop, send
  138	the file/path through an available channel. In Orchestra, the worker gives the result to the
  139	requester; the orchestrator performs user delivery through task-authorized `send_file`.

  141	## One owner of appearance
  142	
  143	In Orchestra, use this skill for HTML, diagrams, animation, and visual explanations. Do not layer
  144	separate design skills over it. Selected techniques live here; saved sources and office templates
  145	are references, not automatic routes. PDF/DOCX/PPTX/XLSX remain tools of their own formats. This
  146	file is self-contained; external skills are not required to run it.
