    8	# Grill Me — stress-test a plan, business, or product
    9	
   10	## Purpose
   11	
   12	Relentlessly stress-test a plan, business model, or product through evidence-based question
   13	frameworks. Find contradictions, blind spots, and untested assumptions. Record the answers.
   14	
   15	## When to invoke
   16	
   17	- "grill me", "stress-test", "criticize this", "find the weak spots"
   18	- "fundamental questions", "contradictions", "stress test"
   19	- "devil's advocate", "take it apart", "poke holes"
   20	- The user shares a plan and wants it challenged
   21	
   22	## Methodology (five question frameworks)
   23	
   24	### 1. Pre-mortem (Gary Klein, HBR 2007)
   25	
   26	Imagine the project has ALREADY failed: "A year has passed; the project is dead. Why?" Shifting
   27	from "what could go wrong" to "what went wrong" improves risk identification by 30% (Wharton,
   28	"Back to the Future", 1989). Future tense → past tense is prospective hindsight.
   29	
   30	### 2. Socratic questioning (six types)
   31	
   32	- **Clarification:** "What do you mean by X?"
   33	- **Probing assumptions:** "Why do you believe X is true? What is it based on?"
   34	- **Probing evidence:** "What data supports this? How do you know?"
   35	- **Exploring alternatives:** "What if the opposite is true? What else could work?"
   36	- **Implications:** "If X is true, what follows?"
   37	- **Meta-questions:** "Why does this question matter at all?"
   38	
   39	### 3. Assumption mapping (Bland & Osterwalder, 2019)
   40	
   41	Use a 2×2 matrix: importance × uncertainty. CB Insights reports that 42% of startups die from
   42	"no market need" — untested demand assumptions.
   43	
   44	- Desirability: "Do customers WANT this?" (most often fatal)
   45	- Feasibility: "CAN we build this?" (less often fatal)
   46	- Viability: "WILL it pay off?" (often ignored)
   47	
   48	### 4. Five Whys (Toyota, Taiichi Ohno)
   49	
   50	For every weak answer, dig deeper. Ask "Why?" across five levels. The goal is the root cause,
   51	not the symptom.
   52	
   53	### 5. Red team (US military → business)
   54	
   55	Adopt the role of a competitor/critic: "If I wanted to destroy this business, how would I do it?"
   56	This is a formal devil's advocate.
   57	
   58	## Process flow
   59	
   60	### Phase 1: Silent analysis
   61	
   62	Read documents relevant to the plan. Build a map:
   63	
   64	- What claims are made?
   65	- What evidence exists?
   66	- What is accepted without verification?
   67	- Where do documents contradict one another?
   68	- Which obvious questions were NOT asked?
   69	
   70	### Phase 2: Pre-mortem
   71	
   72	Start with one question:
   73	
   74	> "Imagine: a year has passed and [project] failed. Customers left and money ran out. What happened? Name the three most likely causes."
   75	
   76	Record the answer. Then continue.
   77	
   78	### Phase 3: Structured grill (10 questions as one batch)
   79	
   80	Choose the 5–7 most relevant categories:
   81	
   82	- 🏗️ **Foundation** — why does this exist, who needs it, what is the evidence?
   83	- 💰 **Unit economics** — CAC, LTV, margin, break-even point
   84	- ⚔️ **Competitors/moat** — who already does this, how are you better, what if X appears tomorrow?
   85	- 📈 **Scale** — what if usage grows 10×, what breaks first, where is the single point of failure?
   86	- ⚠️ **Risks** — vendor, legal, and technical dependencies
   87	- 🎯 **Sales** — who buys, how do you find them, sales cycle, conversion?
   88	- 📋 **Operations** — who does the work, SLA, what if you become ill?
   89	- 🔄 **Contradictions** — "Document A says X; document B says Y — which is true?"
   90	
   91	**Question rules:**
   92	
   93	- Every question cites a CONCRETE fact/document, not a generic "have you considered…?"
   94	- For a contradiction, quote BOTH sides.
   95	- Move from fundamentals to details.
   96	- Match the user's style: direct, without corporate language.
   97	- Give each question its own recommended answer when you have an opinion.
   98	
   99	### Phase 4: Deep dive (Five Whys)
  100	
  101	For weak answers ("no idea", "later", "somehow"), ask Five Whys:
  102	
  103	> "Why is there no SLA?" → "Because I work alone" → "Why alone?" → "Because there is no money for an employee" → "Why?" → "Because there is one customer" → ROOT CAUSE: get a second customer before hiring.
  104	
  105	### Phase 5: Red team
  106	
  107	End with one question:
  108	
  109	> "I am your competitor. I have a $5M budget and a team of ten. How do I kill your business in six months?"
  110	
  111	### Phase 6: Record
  112	
  113	Record everything in a document:
  114	
  115	```markdown
  116	## Grill [topic] — [date]
  117	
  118	### Pre-Mortem: three failure causes
  119	1. ...
  120	
  121	### Questions and answers
  122	**🏗️ Q: [question]**
  123	A: [answer]
  124	
  125	### Assumption Map
  126	| Assumption | Importance | Tested? | How to test |
  127	|---|---|---|---|
  128	
  129	### Unresolved questions
  130	- [ ] ...
  131	
  132	### Red Team: how to kill the business
  133	...
  134	```
  135	
  136	### Phase 7: Next batch
  137	
  138	"Another 10 questions, or go deeper into [topic]?" Each next batch goes deeper into weak spots.
  139	Stop when the user says to stop.
  140	
  141	## Error handling
  142	
  143	- Vague answer ("no idea", "later") → record it + ⚠️ + Five Whys
  144	- Defensive reaction → normal; do not soften. "Better now than a customer in a month."
  145	- No documents → ask verbally and grill the answers
