    2	## Research Method — how to find the TRUTH, not confirm a guess
    3	
    4	Use the methods below where they answer the question or reduce a concrete risk. They are
    5	a reference, not a mandatory sequence or an obligation to produce every artifact.
    6	Check the task's premise and seek counter-evidence for load-bearing conclusions.
    7	
    8	### Step 0 — Frame the question before searching
    9	Restate the task as a structured question. Name explicitly:
   10	- **Context** — the system/component/situation under study
   11	- **Change under test** — the approach, claim, or hypothesis being evaluated
   12	- **Baseline** — what it's compared against (the alternative / status quo)
   13	- **Outcome** — the *measurable* thing that decides the answer
   14	If the question is a "does A beat B on metric M" → this is a comparison, plan a measurement.
   15	If it's "what are the tradeoffs / how does X behave" → this is qualitative, plan multi-source.
   16	A vague question produces duplicated, misdirected searches. Frame first.
   17	
   18	### Step 1 — Hypotheses for causal or disputed questions
   19	For a causal explanation or disputed claim, state the leading hypothesis, a plausible
   20	alternative and evidence that would distinguish or refute them. A lookup of one documented
   21	fact does not require an invented competing hypothesis. Seek disconfirming evidence for
   22	load-bearing explanations rather than collecting only agreement.
   23	
   24	### Step 2 — Investigate: retrieve to ground, think to interpret
   25	Decision rule (retrieve vs. think):
   26	- **RETRIEVE** any external fact: API behavior, version-specific detail, library
   27	  semantics, anything about the world. NEVER answer these from memory.
   28	- **THINK** to decompose the question and interpret what you retrieved.
   29	
   30	Choose search scope from the question: use the exact symbol, error or supplied source
   31	when known; explore broadly when the subject or useful source is still unclear.
   32	Narrow or broaden based on what the results actually establish.
   33	State a concrete retrieval/measurement ceiling before the first call (calls, rounds, time,
   34	or spend). Stop when the outcome is decidable; do not repeat an unchanged query or spend a
   35	round only rephrasing it.
   36	
   37	For the code the task touches: grep/read the ACTUAL source before proposing —
   38	understand before theorizing. Check fallback paths and real call-sites, not just
   39	string matches.
   40	
   41	Judge retrieval relevance before using it. Irrelevant retrieval **poisons**
   42	reasoning — discard it, don't reason on top of it.
   43	
   44	**Prior art — has someone already solved this?**
   45	RUN THIS WHEN the task turns on the behavior of a system outside our code (a protocol, someone
   46	else's service, an unfamiliar library or format) — before you start deep diagnosis or design.
   47	SKIP IT when the answer lives entirely in our own code: a known file/function, a clear repro,
   48	a spec you were handed. Most tasks skip it.
   49	Look for a working solution, not opinions: the project's own source and issue tracker, and
   50	engineering write-ups by people who hit it in production. Timebox it — 2-3 sources, and if
   51	nothing matches your symptom, stop and go diagnose.
   52	Someone else's fix is **tier 2 at best (Step 3): a hypothesis about OUR system, never a verdict.**
   53	Reproduce it here before you act on it, and say in `research.md` whether it matched.
   54	Measured: #143 spent half a day tracing a network stall by hand; the mechanism had been published
   55	a year earlier and surfaced on the first web search — run AFTER the diagnosis, not before it.
   56	
   57	### Step 3 — Rank every source by evidence tier (not by authority or recency)
   58	Rank what a claim rests on, best → worst:
   59	1. **Direct measurement** — you ran it, reproducible numbers (strongest)
   60	2. **Primary source** — official docs, source code, the spec, the actual paper
   61	3. **≥2 independent secondary sources agreeing**
   62	4. **A single secondary source** (one blog / one thread)
   63	5. **Your own memory with no source** — weakest, frequently fabricated (don't assert it)
   64	
   65	Apply the tier to EVERY source, including famous ones. Big-name ≠ correct.
   66	New ≠ better than a foundational primary source. If a recent claim contradicts a
   67	primary source, flag the conflict — don't silently prefer the newer one.
   68	
   69	### Step 4 — Seek counter-evidence, then decompose & verify
   70	- **Actively search for refutation** of your leading hypothesis (Step 1 falsifier).
   71	  A second source that *agrees* is weak; a search that *tries to break* your claim is strong.
   72	- Before writing a finding, list it as **atomic claims** — each independently
   73	  checkable, self-contained (no dangling "it"/"this"), qualifiers preserved
   74	  (negation, numbers, dates, "only/always/sometimes"). Dropping a qualifier changes truth.
   75	- For each atomic claim, confirm it has a **source you actually opened** or a
   76	  measurement you actually ran. No source/measurement → it's a hypothesis, label it so.
   77	- Keep that supporting source or measurement next to each claim in the report; a candidate
   78	  list or discovery snippet routes reading but does not establish a substantive finding.
   79	
   80	### Step 5 — Experiment when the task needs empirical proof
   81	- Run alternatives when the decision depends on empirical behavior. An explicit requirement,
   82	  source-code contradiction, unacceptable side effect or unavailable prerequisite can rule an
   83	  option out without executing it. Distinguish measured results from source-based reasoning
   84	  and untested hypotheses; do not claim an unrun experiment succeeded or failed.
   85	- Define **metrics + pass/fail BEFORE running.** Do not move goalposts after seeing results (p-hacking).
   86	- Before a full run, pilot the complete measurement path on 2–3 candidates. If candidates can
   87	  change external state, give blind judges each candidate's measured side effects/artifact diffs;
   88	  transcript-only scoring is invalid because real actions look fabricated.
   89	- Run experiments in isolation, not production. Choose repeats based on noise and decision risk,
   90	  not a fixed count; a deterministic reproduction may need only one run.
   91	  **Do not put large files in `/tmp`** — check `findmnt /tmp` first: on the laptop it is tmpfs,
   92	  so "files on disk" are RAM you are taking from other agents, and a reboot erases them.
   93	  Models, DB snapshots and datasets go on a real disk (`data/`, project dir); keep `/tmp`
   94	  for the scripts themselves.
   95	- Record raw numbers/outputs/errors verbatim.
   96	- For every reproduction, record the revision, outcome-sensitive effective configuration/input
   97	  (including environment/model when relevant), and observable end effect in the production-shaped
   98	  runtime; command status or an intermediate success is not evidence.
   99	- A valid counter-example can refute a universal claim. For noisy empirical comparisons,
  100	  investigate reproducibility and uncertainty before changing the conclusion.
  101	- **Multi-link mechanism whose first link can fail by returning empty/no-op instead of raising
  102	  → prove the OBSERVABLE END EFFECT, in the production-shaped runtime, and never accept an
  103	  intermediate "this step worked" as evidence.** The highest-risk shape of that first link is an
  104	  OPTIONAL API — `get_extra_info(...)`, `getattr(x, name, default)`, an optional field — which
  105	  answers `None` instead of raising when it cannot serve you. If your change alters where such a
  106	  value comes from, first prove the OLD path returned something non-`None` in the production
  107	  runtime; otherwise you are "fixing" a path that was already dead. A silently-degrading first link hides every
  108	  defect behind it, so each fix only reveals the next one and the whole chain can be dead while
  109	  looking healthy. Probe in the same configuration production uses — a standalone probe on
  110	  different defaults measures the probe. Measured (#237): uvloop returned `fd=None`, which hid a
  111	  blocking `proc.wait()`, an invalid `:` in FDNAME, a terminal oracle watching an event type that
  112	  does not exist for that runtime, a stale `cli_pid`, and a resume query excluding the row —
  113	  six defects, one behind another, in a mechanism believed to work after a "successful" restart.
  114	
  115	### Preserve the research result
  116	
  117	The knowledge module owns what belongs in the report, KB and personal notes.
  118	
  119	### Do NOT (each is a measured failure mode):
  120	- **Do NOT seek confirmation.** Go looking for what proves you WRONG. Step-by-step
  121	  reasoning locks in an early wrong guess — counter it deliberately.
  122	- **Do NOT flip your answer just because challenged/pushed.** Flip only when NEW
  123	  EVIDENCE warrants it. Caving to pushback = instability, not reasoning.
  124	- **Do NOT cite a source you didn't open.** Models can fabricate references from memory,
  125	  including when search is available. If you didn't fetch it, you may not cite it.
  126	- **Do NOT paraphrase without analysis.** A summary is not a finding. Decompose into
  127	  atomic claims and verify each. Grounding + traceability over fluency.
  128	- **Do NOT trust a source because it's famous or recent.** Rank by susceptibility to
  129	  bias, not by brand or date. Corroborate big names too.
  130	- **Do NOT reason on top of irrelevant retrieval.** Bad retrieval poisons the chain — discard it.
  131	- **Do NOT move the goalposts.** Define pass/fail before running; don't redefine "success" after seeing results.
  132	- **Do NOT state memory as fact.** "I think" / "typically" / "should be" without a
  133	  source is a hypothesis — label it UNCERTAIN, or go verify it.
  134	- **Do NOT assume one source = truth.** The cited source can be outdated, disputed,
  135	  or cherry-picked; real questions have conflicting evidence. Present the conflict.
  136	
  137	### Effort scaling (don't over-ceremony a trivial lookup):
  138	- **One fact** → inspect its authoritative source and verify relevance; stop when answered.
  139	- **Comparison** → measure when feasible; otherwise compare relevant evidence and state limits.
  140	- **Contested or high-risk claims** → seek independent counter-evidence and test load-bearing
  141	  assumptions. Choose depth by consequences and uncertainty, not a fixed source count.
