🧩The long-context story

The single most practically important difference. 4.7 traded long-range retrieval for agentic coding; 4.8 clawed some back.

Read this: MRCR v2 needle-in-haystack collapsed from 4.6→4.7 (78.3%→32.2% at 1M tokens), attributed to the new tokenizer. Anthropic's own card admits 4.6 + extended thinking beats 4.7 on multi-needle retrieval. No MRCR number was published for 4.8 — recovery is inferred from the GraphWalks successor benchmark, where 4.8 jumps to 68.1% @1M vs 4.7's 40.3%. Source: wentuo.ai MRCR analysis

🧠When to use which

Reach for 4.6 when…

Long-context / RAG

Multi-document retrieval, huge codebases read end-to-end, anything that lives near the 1M window. It's the only version with verified strong needle-in-haystack at depth, and the old tokenizer makes it cheapest per task. Tradeoff: weaker agentic coding.

Reach for 4.7 when…

Steerable agents

If you've pinned it and tuned prompts around it: best reward-hacking resistance with an anti-hack prompt (12.5%), lowest prompt-injection false-neg (0.07%), strong tool-call efficiency. Mostly superseded by 4.8 — little reason to start new work here.

Reach for 4.8 when…

Default choice

Agentic coding, parallel subagents, cost-sensitive throughput (3× cheaper fast mode, fewer output tokens), and anywhere you need the model to flag its own mistakes. Watch for social sycophancy and don't rely on it for deep 1M-token retrieval.

One-line verdict: 4.8 for almost everything new — except deep long-context retrieval, where 4.6 still wins outright.
